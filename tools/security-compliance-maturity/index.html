Security & Compliance Maturity

A maturity ladder across the six NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 functions, Govern included, scored on evidence rather than stated policy, plus the common trap of mistaking a compliance certification for actual maturity.

A self-assessment framework, not a substitute for an independent audit. Internal scoring is useful for prioritization; external validation is what an auditor, customer, or regulator will rely on.
Framework

Maturity ladder, mapped to NIST CSF 2.0 functions

Five levels across the six NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 functions. Score each function separately; most organizations are unevenly mature across them, which is itself useful information. Govern sits underneath the other five, added in CSF 2.0 specifically because a technically mature program with no executive-level ownership or risk strategy is a real and common gap, not a hypothetical one.

How to use this

Scoring honestly, and what to do with the result

The assessment only has value if the scoring is honest, which is harder than it sounds when the person scoring it is also accountable for the result.

How to score it

Score against evidence, not intention. "We have a policy" is not Level 3 if the policy isn't followed; it's evidence of intent, not practice. Ask for the artifact, the access review log, the tested restore record, the tabletop exercise notes, before accepting a self-reported level.

What to do with a low score

A Level 1 or 2 in any function is not itself an emergency; it's a prioritization input. Rank functions by business impact if that function fails, not by how uncomfortable the low score is, and fund the highest-impact gap first rather than the easiest one to fix.

Common pitfall

Compliance certification as a false signal of maturity

A frequent gap between what a certification confirms and what an organization assumes it confirms.

What a certification confirms

SOC 2 or ISO 27001 confirms that defined controls existed and were followed during the specific audit period the certification covers, evaluated against a scope the organization itself proposed.

What it doesn't confirm

That the scope covered every system that matters, that the maturity level is sustained rather than a point-in-time snapshot assembled for the audit, or that a novel attack pattern outside the certified controls would be caught. Treat certification as a floor, not a ceiling.

Why NIST CSF functions as a crosswalk

The practical reason to run this assessment at all, beyond the framework being well known.

What it enables

Most major regulatory frameworks and industry standards reference or heavily overlap with CSF 2.0's six functions, Govern especially, since regulators and customer security questionnaires increasingly ask the governance question directly: who owns this, and how is it reported to leadership. A single NIST CSF assessment gives a reasonable starting map for gap analysis against HIPAA, PCI DSS, or a customer security questionnaire, without redoing the assessment from scratch for each one.

Where the crosswalk breaks down

A healthcare or payments-specific regulation has requirements that don't map cleanly onto any single CSF function, a specific breach notification timeline, a specific cardholder data handling rule. The crosswalk gets you most of the way, not all the way; the remaining gap has to be checked directly against the specific regulation's text.

How this differs from Zero-Trust Maturity

Two maturity pages that look similar in structure but answer different questions, worth running both rather than picking one.

This page scores a broad governance and lifecycle posture using a widely recognized standard, useful for board conversations, audit readiness, and mapping against specific regulatory obligations. Zero-Trust Maturity scores a specific architectural posture across six dimensions purpose-built around identity, segmentation, and blast radius, useful for engineering and security architecture decisions. The two should tell different stories about the same organization, not duplicate the same story in different language. A team scoring well on this page's Protect function can still score poorly on zero-trust segmentation, and that gap is exactly the kind of thing worth knowing rather than averaging away.
