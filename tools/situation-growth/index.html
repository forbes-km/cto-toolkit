The Company Is Growing Rapidly

Growth hides problems until it does not. Structures, processes, and systems that were fine at fifty people or a thousand customers fail non-linearly, and they fail at the moment the organization can least afford it. The job is to see which constraint breaks next and to change it before it does, without building the bureaucracy of a company three times the current size. This page sequences the decisions and links to the pages that hold the detail.

Questions to ask

Questions that reveal what breaks next

Growth failures are predictable if someone is looking. These are what to look at.

  • Which single system, team, or person is the constraint on delivery today, and what is the next one after it
  • How many people report to each engineering manager, and how many managers have never managed before
  • What is the ratio of new hires to people who have been here more than a year, per team
  • Which of the current processes exist because someone decided, and which exist because nobody did
  • How long does it take a new engineer to ship to production, and what is that number's trend
  • What is the change failure rate and recovery time, and is anyone looking at them weekly
  • Which architectural decisions have already been made three different ways by three different teams
  • What breaks first at ten times the current volume, and has anyone tested it
  • What does the next tier of customers or the next market require in security, compliance, or residency that the company does not have today
  • Which of the founders' informal roles need to become someone's actual job
The trap

Hiring as the fix for every constraint

When the company is growing, the default response to any delivery problem is headcount. It is the fix that requires no structural decision, and it makes most structural problems worse.

What it looks like

Delivery slows. Twenty engineers are hired. Onboarding consumes the senior engineers, span of control passes ten, the architecture drifts because nobody has time to review anything, and delivery per engineer halves. The headcount doubled and the output did not move. The board sees a cost line that grew faster than revenue.

The counter-move

Find the binding constraint before hiring into it, using the approach in Systems Thinking for CTOs and Understanding the System Before Changing It. Fix structure first: leveling, team boundaries, a platform layer, a review path. Then hire into a shape that can absorb people. Watch delivery per engineer as a leading indicator, and be willing to slow hiring when it falls.

Outputs

What should exist at 30, 90, and 180 days

Structure that is ready before the people arrive, and numbers that show whether it is working.
