Agile and SAFe

What agile requires versus what most transformations install instead, and where SAFe specifically earns its overhead at scale versus where it becomes its own kind of theater.

The four values, and what commonly gets installed instead

Each value in the agile manifesto requires something real. Most organizations install a process substitute that satisfies the ritual without the underlying substance.

ValueWhat it requiresWhat commonly gets installed instead
Individuals and interactions over processes and toolsTrust, and a team empowered to change its own processA fixed ceremony calendar treated as the definition of agile itself
Working software over comprehensive documentationFrequent, real integration and deploymentSprint demos of work that isn't deployed, satisfying the ritual without the substance
Customer collaboration over contract negotiationDirect, frequent contact between the team and the people using what they buildA product owner as the sole interface, recreating the exact handoff agile was meant to remove
Responding to change over following a planA backlog genuinely re-prioritized as new information arrivesA locked sprint commitment defended against any change, optimizing for predictability over responsiveness

Why "doing agile" and "being agile" diverge

The gap that determines whether the ceremonies are earning their keep.

Being agile

The team changes what it does based on what it learns, mid-sprint if warranted, and treats each ceremony as a tool it owns and can modify or drop the moment it stops earning its keep.

Doing agile

Standup, retro, planning, and review run on schedule regardless of whether they're producing value, treated as compliance requirements rather than tools the team controls. The tell: ask when the team last changed its own process based on a retro, and get silence.

Where SAFe genuinely helps

A real coordination problem exists at scale, and SAFe was built to address it, whatever else gets said about the framework.

The real problem it addresses

Coordinating dependencies across many teams building toward a shared release or shared platform, where informal coordination genuinely breaks down past a certain team count and someone has to own cross-team sequencing explicitly rather than leaving it to hope.

Roughly where it starts to matter

Often cited around 50 to 125 people, five to twelve teams, though the real trigger is dependency density between teams, not headcount alone. Ten independent teams with no shared release need none of this; four teams shipping one product together might.

3

Where SAFe becomes its own theater

The same framework, installed for a coordination problem the organization doesn't have.

The failure mode

The framework's ceremonies, PI planning, the various sync meetings, the layered roles, get installed wholesale regardless of whether the organization's actual coordination problem matches what SAFe was built to solve, producing ceremony overhead that outweighs the coordination it buys.

The tell

Ask what specific coordination failure a given SAFe ceremony prevents. If the honest answer is "we don't know, this is just what SAFe says to do," the ceremony is cargo cult, not a solution to a problem the organization has.

4

Scaling down deliberately, not just up

The adoption decision most organizations skip: choosing which pieces to install and which to leave out.

Practice

Apply only the specific mechanisms that address a genuine, named coordination problem, and skip the rest. A framework adopted partially and deliberately produces less overhead than either the full framework or no coordination structure at all.

Common failure mode

Adopting the framework "properly" because partial adoption feels like doing it wrong, which installs overhead for coordination problems the organization doesn't have, simply because the framework bundles them together with the ones it does.

Checklist

Signs agile or SAFe has become theater

Patterns that show up when process is running on schedule but no longer producing what it's meant to.

  • Ceremonies run on schedule regardless of whether the team can name what each one is currently producing
  • The team has never changed its own process based on a retrospective
  • Story points or velocity get reported upward as if comparable across different teams
  • SAFe ceremonies are followed without anyone able to name the specific coordination failure each one prevents
  • "Being agile" and "following the ceremony calendar" are treated as the same thing by leadership
Reference only. The right level of process depends on team count, dependency density, and organizational maturity; there is no universally correct amount of ceremony.
