EA Maturity Assessment

A structured baseline across six dimensions of enterprise architecture maturity, with a working scoring calculator and the diagnostic patterns that show up when specific dimensions diverge from each other.

This calculator implements the real six-dimension framework and scoring scale. The full facilitator guide covers running a proper multi-scorer assessment, reconciliation sessions, and building a twelve-month roadmap from the result, available through direct engagement.
1

What this assessment is for

Produces two things and nothing more: a defensible baseline across six dimensions, and a starting position for a twelve-month improvement plan. It doesn't produce a grade.

Most useful when
  • A new EA leader takes over the function
  • An organization is deciding whether to invest in EA at all
  • An established function has plateaued and needs a structured look at why
The scoring scale
  • 1, Absent: no recognizable version exists
  • 2, Emerging: exists in pockets, driven by specific individuals, not reliably available
  • 3, Defined: named, documented, applied consistently in most cases
  • 4, Managed: measured, reviewed, improves in response to data
  • 5, Optimized: integrated into business decision-making with evidence of enterprise-wide impact
Most functions should target 3 to 4 across all six, not 5 everywhere. A 5 in every dimension usually signals over-investment relative to actual risk, not genuine excellence.
Framework

The six dimensions

Each answers a different question about the function. A function can be strong in one and weak in another; that unevenness is the useful signal, not noise to average away.

DimensionWhat it asks
Strategy AlignmentDoes EA's work connect to the strategy the enterprise is pursuing? The dimension most commonly rated optimistically.
GovernanceAre architectural decisions made through a process that scales, with exceptions tracked and reviews credible to delivery teams?
Domain CoverageDoes the function cover the domains EA should cover, or is it a strong application-architecture function playing above its weight class?
Delivery IntegrationDoes EA produce better decisions in delivery, or operate as a parallel track delivery works around?
Talent & Operating ModelIs the function organized to be sustainable: roles, succession, sponsor relationships, demand management?
Artifacts & ToolingDoes the function maintain what it produces, at a cost proportionate to the value? Scored last and carefully; the easiest dimension to over-invest in.
Interactive

Score your own function

Rate each dimension 1 to 5. Runs entirely in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere or stored. For a real assessment, score independently across 2 to 4 scorers before reconciling, per the facilitator guidance below, this calculator is for a single scorer's pass.

2

Running it as a real assessment

This page's calculator is a single-scorer pass. A real assessment is deliberately multi-evaluator, same principle as the vendor scorecard, for the same reason.

Select scorers

The EA leader scores, plus two to four others selected for angle of view, not seniority: a senior delivery leader, a business sponsor who consumes EA output, an outside solution architect, optionally a prior EA leader. Avoid scorers who have a position to defend.

Score, then reconcile

Each scorer completes the assessment independently, same briefing, same scale, no discussion, forty-five minutes. Then a ninety-minute session, dimension by dimension: where scores diverge by more than one point, discuss it. The discussion is the data, and persistent divergence is itself a finding worth preserving, not something to average away.

The overall average is the least interesting number in the assessment, useful for trending year over year, not for conclusions in any single pass. Individual indicators scored 1 or 2 are what a roadmap should be built around.
Reference

Diagnostic patterns the calculator flags

The average hides the finding. These are the score shapes that recur, what each usually means, and what to do about it before building a roadmap.

PatternWhat it usually meansFirst move
Artifacts & Tooling high, Delivery Integration lowA documentation function. The models are beautiful and delivery teams do not consult themStop producing artifacts for a quarter; embed architects in the two most important delivery streams and measure whether decisions change
Strategy Alignment high, everything else lowThe EA leader is trusted by executives and the function has no machinery beneath that trustGovernance first: a lightweight review path with a written exception process, so the trust converts into decisions that stick
Governance high, Delivery Integration lowA gate. Reviews happen, delivery teams route around them, and the exception log is where the real architecture livesMove review earlier and make it evidence-based; see Architecture Review Board
Domain Coverage low, all else moderateA strong application architecture group called EA. Data, security, and business architecture are uncoveredName the uncovered domains explicitly and decide whether to staff them, partner for them, or state that they are out of scope
Talent & Operating Model at 1 or 2 with anything at 4Key-person risk. The function's maturity is one resignation away from a 2Succession and demand management before any further capability investment
Uniform 3sEither an honest, balanced, mid-maturity function or a single scorer who did not want to commit. The multi-scorer process tells you whichRun the reconciliation session before drawing any conclusion
Worked example

Reading one real score pattern

The score that most often surprises the EA leader who runs it on their own function.

The scores

Strategy Alignment 2, Governance 3, Domain Coverage 3, Delivery Integration 2, Talent & Operating Model 2, Artifacts & Tooling 4. Average 2.7. The EA leader's own pre-assessment estimate was 3.5. The function has a mature capability model, a current application catalog, and a well-maintained standards library. Delivery leaders, asked in the reconciliation session, could not name a decision the function had changed in the past year.

The reading

This is the library pattern: the highest-scoring dimension is the one that is easiest to over-invest in, and the two lowest are the ones that determine whether the function matters. The artifacts are not the problem, but the next dollar should not go to them. The twelve-month roadmap that came out of it moved one architect into the payments modernization program full time, replaced the monthly architecture forum with a review attached to real design work, and set one measurable target: three documented decisions per quarter where a delivery team changed course because of an EA input. A year later the artifact score had not moved and the function's budget had been protected in a cost cut, which is the outcome that matters.

Method

Turning the result into a twelve-month roadmap

The assessment produces a baseline. The roadmap is what justifies having run it.

QuarterFocusEvidence at quarter end
Q1The single lowest-scoring dimension that determines relevance, almost always Delivery Integration or Strategy AlignmentOne delivery stream with an architect embedded; one strategic initiative with an EA-authored options paper that executives used
Q2Governance that delivery teams experience as help: review path, exception process, decision recordsReviews happening on real design work, exception log with owners, first ADRs published
Q3Operating model: demand intake, roles, succession, sponsor cadenceA written demand process, a named successor for the EA lead, a quarterly sponsor review on the calendar
Q4Domain coverage decision and re-score with the same scorersAn explicit statement of which domains EA covers; a second assessment showing movement in the dimensions targeted and, ideally, no movement in Artifacts

Re-score annually with the same scorers where possible. The trend across two or three years is the first point at which the average becomes meaningful; in any single year, the individual indicators at 1 and 2 are the whole story.

Common pitfall

Scoring on intent

The assessment is only as good as the honesty of the scoring, and the scorer with the most at stake is usually the EA leader.

What it looks like

Governance is scored a 4 because a review process is documented and a board meets monthly. Nobody checks how many designs went through it, how many exceptions were granted, or whether any decision was ever reversed by it. The score reflects the process that exists on paper, and the roadmap built on it fixes the wrong dimension.

The counter-move

Every score of 3 or above needs an artifact: the review log, the exception register, the decision that changed, the sponsor's own words. If the artifact cannot be produced in the reconciliation session, the score drops to a 2 and the missing artifact becomes a roadmap item. This is the same discipline the Zero-Trust Maturity and Security & Compliance Maturity assessments apply, for the same reason.
