Technology Decision Rights

Making decision ownership explicit with a RACI-style model, because most executive conflict is really an undefined decision right wearing a personality conflict's clothes.

See Working with the CEO for the escalation guidance this model formalizes. The table below is illustrative; your organization's actual columns and rows will differ.

1

Why explicit rights beat "we'll figure it out when it comes up"

The alternative to a stated model isn't no model, it's a fresh political negotiation every time the same category of decision recurs.

What explicit rights do

A decision made once, in advance, about who owns what. A live disagreement becomes a matter of checking the model rather than relitigating it from scratch with whoever happens to be in the room and how forcefully they argue.

What ambiguity produces

The same category of decision gets resolved differently every time depending on who's present, which erodes trust in whoever "won" and guarantees the losing side argues it again next time, since nothing was settled.

2

Accountable is not the same as Responsible

The distinction most RACI exercises blur, which is exactly where they stop working.

The distinction

Responsible executes the work. Accountable owns the outcome and has final say when consulted input conflicts. Exactly one person should be Accountable per decision; if two people are both marked A, the model hasn't resolved anything, it's just documented the ambiguity.

The common mistake

Marking multiple people Accountable to avoid conflict in the room where the RACI gets built. This doesn't remove the conflict, it just moves it to the moment the decision has to be made, when the stakes are higher and the room is smaller.

3

Building the table for your organization

The example above is a starting shape, not a template to adopt unchanged.

Build it in a room with the actual stakeholders, not alone. Force explicit disagreement about at least one row before finalizing; if nobody disagrees with anything, the exercise probably wasn't done honestly, since real organizations have real tension over at least a few of these decisions. Revisit it annually, or after any reorg, since org changes shift decision rights even when nobody remembers to update the document.

4

When a decision doesn't map to any existing row

A gap in the RACI is information, not a reason to improvise quietly and move on.

What to do

Decide deliberately whether this is new, add a row, on the record, with the same rigor as the original exercise, or a variant of an existing row whose language was too narrow. Either way, the document changes; the gap doesn't just get patched over informally and forgotten.

Common failure mode

Treating each gap as a one-off exception handled quietly in the moment. The RACI slowly becomes less accurate than the day it was built, and by the second year it covers less of what's happening than it did on day one, without anyone deciding that should happen.

5

When Consulted input gets ignored

The most common source of quiet resentment in a working RACI, and it's usually a misunderstanding of what Consulted promises.

What Consulted means

The Accountable party has to hear the input before deciding, not adopt it. Consulted is not a veto, and treating it like one defeats the entire purpose of having a single accountable owner who can move a decision forward.

When it's a real problem

The same Consulted party's input gets dismissed without explanation, repeatedly, on the same category of decision. That's not the RACI failing, it's a signal the Accountable assignment itself may be wrong, or that the underlying relationship needs the direct conversation described on Working with the CEO or Working with the CFO.

6

A worked disagreement

What the mechanics above look like resolving a real, live tension instead of a hypothetical one.

Product wants to ship a new vendor integration fast, a competitor just launched something similar and the sales team is asking daily. Security flags a data residency concern with the vendor's hosting: customer data would leave the region a key enterprise contract requires it to stay in.

Checking the table: major vendor selection has the CTO Accountable, CISO and Product both Consulted, Engineering Responsible. The CISO's flag isn't a veto, but the CTO has to weigh it, not note it and move on. In this case, the CTO goes back to the CISO for the specific requirement, then negotiates a contractual data residency clause with the vendor rather than blocking the integration outright. The integration ships two weeks later than Product wanted. Security's actual concern gets addressed rather than either overridden or allowed to stall the decision indefinitely. Nobody has to relitigate who had the final call, because the table already said so before the disagreement ever started.

Checklist

Signs decision rights are still effectively undefined

Having a document isn't the same as having working decision rights. These patterns show up when the model exists on paper but isn't load-bearing.

  • The same category of decision gets escalated differently depending on who's involved
  • Two people both believe they're Accountable for the same decision
  • The RACI was built once and never revisited despite a reorg since
  • Decisions get made in hallway conversations that bypass the stated model entirely
  • No one can locate the document when a live disagreement happens
  • A Consulted party's input has been dismissed without explanation on the same category of decision more than once
