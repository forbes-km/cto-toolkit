Technology Strategy During a Downturn

What to do when the CEO says cut technology spend twenty percent by next quarter. The distinction between a cut that costs the business less than it saves and one that doesn't.

This is the execution playbook for a cut. See Technology Capital Allocation for the pre-ranked priority list this page assumes already exists.
Reference

Bad cuts versus better cuts

Not every reduction in spend is equivalent. Some remove cost with almost no value loss; others remove cost by removing value.

Across-the-board headcount reductionsLow-value initiatives already flagged as candidates before the pressure arrived

1

Don't confuse reducing spend with reducing value

The two aren't the same thing, and the best cuts are the ones where they diverge most.

Execute these first

Cloud rightsizing, duplicate SaaS tooling consolidation, and unused capacity elimination reduce spend with close to zero value loss. These should always be executed before anything with real value attached, and they're usually available in weeks, not quarters.

The trap

An across-the-board percentage cut applied uniformly treats a duplicate analytics tool and a security control as equally expendable, which they are not, and guarantees the cut removes real value somewhere it didn't need to.

2

Communicating the cut without destroying morale

How the reduction is explained matters almost as much as what's cut.

Practice

Be specific about what's being cut and why, tied to the pre-ranked priority list, so the team understands this was a deliberate choice made against known criteria, not a panicked reaction to a number that arrived from finance.

Common failure mode

Vague, repeated "doing more with less" messaging with no specifics, which reads as either incompetence or dishonesty to the people doing the work, and erodes trust well beyond whatever the cut itself cost.

3

What not to cut even under real pressure

Some categories aren't discretionary line items, whatever the topline number demands.

Security, reliability, and necessary architectural remediation don't reduce cost when cut, they defer it at compounding interest. A downturn is exactly the moment the organization can least afford a preventable incident stacked on top of everything else already under pressure. If these categories appear on a cut list, the list needs to be rebuilt, not defended.

Signs the cut was done badly

Patterns that show up when a budget reduction was executed under pressure without the discipline this page describes.

  • Cuts were applied as a uniform percentage rather than against a pre-ranked priority list
  • Security or reliability budget was reduced specifically to hit a topline number
  • The team was told to do more with less without specifics on what was deprioritized
  • Innovation bets were cut without applying the kill criteria that should have already existed for them
  • No one has revisited the cuts to confirm they didn't quietly become permanent once the downturn ended
Reference only. Reasonable cut targets and sequencing depend on company runway, market position, and how long the downturn is expected to last.
