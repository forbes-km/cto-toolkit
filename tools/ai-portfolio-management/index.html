AI Portfolio Management

Which AI initiatives should get built, scored and classified with the same discipline applied to the rest of the application estate, rather than funded by enthusiasm or the fear of being left behind.

See Application Portfolio Management for the same underlying discipline applied to existing applications, and AI Architecture Map, AI Governance Map, and AI FinOps Map for the technical, governance, and cost detail behind any initiative that clears this screen.

Scoring dimensions and classification

Nine dimensions feed the score; four classifications follow from it. The value is in forcing the same questions to be asked of every initiative, so the loudest pitch can't skip the screen. Precision in the score itself matters less.

DimensionWhat it asks
Business valueWhat outcome does this move, and how much
FeasibilityCan this be built with current capability and technology
Data readinessDoes the required data exist, at the needed quality, with clear rights to use it
RiskRegulatory, reputational, and model-behavior risk if this goes wrong
Adoption likelihoodWill the people this is built for use it
Time to valueHow long until the outcome in row one is realized, which is a later date than launch
Operating costThe ongoing cost to run this, on top of the cost to build it
DifferentiationDoes this create an advantage competitors can't quickly copy
ReversibilityHow expensive is it to unwind this if it doesn't work out
ClassificationMeaning
ScaleProven value and feasibility; fund it fully and remove obstacles
IncubatePromising but unproven on one or more dimensions; fund a bounded pilot with a defined checkpoint
WatchNot ready to fund, but conditions could change; revisit on a set schedule rather than forgetting about it
KillFails on a gating dimension or has passed its own kill criteria; stop funding it explicitly and say so
1

Feasibility and data readiness gate the score before anything else contributes to it

Averaging all nine dimensions into one number lets a high-value, technically infeasible idea pass through if it scores generously elsewhere.

The right sequence

Screen on feasibility and data readiness first. If the data doesn't exist at usable quality, or the model risk is unacceptable regardless of upside, the initiative doesn't reach a business-value conversation at all. Gating dimensions eliminate; they don't get averaged away.

What a single blended score hides

A compelling business case with a fatal data gap can still produce a respectable average score if the other eight dimensions are strong, which is exactly how organizations end up funding pilots that were never going to work, discovered only after real money and credibility were spent.

2

Reversibility deserves its own weight

Not all AI bets carry the same downside if they fail, and the classification should reflect that.

Why it matters

A pilot built on a vendor API is cheap to unwind. A model embedded into a core workflow customers now depend on is expensive to unwind. Two initiatives with identical business-value scores warrant different treatment if one is trivially reversible and the other isn't.

Where this gets missed

Treating an irreversible bet with the same enthusiasm as a reversible pilot because both scored well on value, without ever separately asking what it costs to walk away if the initial results don't hold up at scale.

3

Kill criteria set before the bet starts

AI initiatives are unusually prone to surviving on prestige rather than results, which makes advance kill criteria more important here than almost anywhere else in the portfolio.

Practice

Define, before funding, the specific result that would trigger a move to Watch or Kill, a metric, a threshold, a date. Write it down where it can't be quietly renegotiated once the team is emotionally invested in the outcome.

Common failure mode

An initiative that's been "six months from proving itself" for over a year, kept alive because killing an AI project carries a different kind of organizational embarrassment than killing an ordinary feature, independent of what the results show.

Interactive

Score an initiative

Score all nine dimensions 1 to 5. Feasibility and data readiness are hard gates; a low score on either kills the initiative regardless of everything else. Runs entirely in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere or stored.

Stage gates with evidence, not enthusiasm

Each gate asks for evidence that did not exist at the previous one. Durations are typical, not rules.

GateEvidence required to passTypical kill signal
1. Feasibility spike, 2 to 6 weeksAn offline evaluation on real, representative data with a written rubric; confirmed rights to use the data; a rough cost per taskQuality cannot beat the current manual baseline on the evaluation set
2. Pilot, 8 to 12 weeksReal users on real work; measured outcome against a baseline; cost per successful task including human review; a named business ownerUsers route around it, or the review burden eats the time saved
3. ScaleService levels, monitoring for quality drift, a human escalation path, incident runbook, unit economics inside targetCost per task grows with volume instead of falling
4. Operate and revisitQuarterly re-scoring against the same nine dimensions; model and vendor changes re-evaluatedA cheaper or better option makes the build redundant

6

Measure value, not usage

The most common way AI portfolios lie to themselves is by reporting activity as if it were outcome.

What to measure

Outcome per task against the pre-AI baseline, such as minutes per claim, first-contact resolution, or error rate, and the fully loaded cost per successful task, including human review, retries, and model spend. Report both monthly for anything past gate 2.

What misleads

Seats licensed, prompts sent, or share of employees who tried the tool. All of them rise while the outcome stays flat, and they are what most dashboards show by default.

Checklist

Signs AI portfolio governance isn't real

Patterns that show up when AI initiatives are being funded by momentum rather than by the same discipline applied elsewhere in the technology portfolio.

  • No AI initiative has ever been formally moved to Kill despite some producing no measurable result
  • Feasibility and data readiness were never checked before funding, only the business case
  • The same initiative has been described as close to proving itself for well over a year
  • No one can name what's currently in Watch status or when it's due for reassessment
  • AI investment decisions bypass the standard capital allocation process applied to everything else
  • Initiatives pass from pilot to scale without a measured outcome against a pre-AI baseline
  • The Risk score was set before checking which rules apply to the use case, including the revised EU AI Act dates and US state laws
