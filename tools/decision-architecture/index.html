Decision Architecture

The structure behind the 52 pages: six stages, plus the pages that don't fit a stage at all.

Technology leadership isn't the optimization of technology. It's the design of an organizational system that lets technology create business value while controlling risk, complexity, cost, and friction. That system has a shape: strategy sets direction, an operating model turns direction into how decisions get made, architecture and governance constrain what gets built and how, execution is where it happens, and measurement tells you whether any of it worked, then feeds back into strategy. The loop back to strategy follows Deming's plan-do-check-act cycle; the split between governance and operating model follows COBIT's separation of governing from managing. Every page below sits somewhere in that shape, or explains why it doesn't.

How · PrOACT

Making one decision well

Hammond, Keeney, and Raiffa's PrOACT framework (Smart Choices, 1999, building on Howard Raiffa's earlier decision analysis work) is the standard sequence, run inside whichever of the six stages the decision currently sits in.

PrOACT stepWhat it looks likeWhere it lives here
Frame the ProblemState what's being decided, and what's explicitly out of scopeStrategy, Technology Strategy
ObjectivesState what you're optimizing for before you look at options, so the options don't retroactively define the goalStrategy, Business Case Calculator
AlternativesReal options on the table, not the one you already prefer plus a straw manGovernance, ADRs & Roadmapping
ConsequencesConfidence-graded evidence for what each alternative producesFoundational, Epistemology & Research Methods
TradeoffsExplicit scoring against the same criteria, so the tradeoff is visible instead of hidden inside a gut callStrategy, Vendor Evaluation
ChooseA named, accountable owner makes the callOperating Model, Technology Decision Rights
Implement and monitorShip it, then check whether it produced what the objectives step said it shouldExecution and Measurement, Engineering Metrics
The six stages above answer where a decision's supporting content lives. PrOACT answers how you make any one decision well, regardless of which stage it's in. A CTO doing both, using the stage map to find the right page and the PrOACT sequence to reason through the call, is the difference this page exists to make explicit.

Epistemology is about how you evaluate claims across every stage above, not a stage itself. Systems Thinking is the lens, and Seeing the System Before Changing It is the diagnostic that lens produces, both apply regardless of which stage the decision sits in. The relationship pages aren't a phase you complete, they're continuously active regardless of which stage the actual work is in.
