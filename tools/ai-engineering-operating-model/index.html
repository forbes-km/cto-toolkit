AI and the Engineering Operating Model

What AI-assisted coding is doing to how engineering work gets done: skill development, code review, and how to measure productivity when the tool itself is part of the output. Distinct from deciding which AI initiatives to build.

See AI Portfolio Management for which AI initiatives are worth funding, and Engineering Metrics for the outcome measures this page argues should replace raw output volume.

What changes, and what doesn't

AI-assisted coding shifts specific parts of the job and leaves others alone. Treating it as a uniform speedup misreads what's happening.

ChangesDoesn't change
Speed of first-draft code and boilerplate generationThe cost of a wrong architectural decision
What code review has to look forWho's accountable for what ships
How junior engineers first encounter patternsThe need for architectural judgment on anything non-routine
Where the bottleneck in the delivery pipeline sitsThe value of debugging intuition built through direct repetition
1

AI productivity does not automatically equal more story points

The most common measurement mistake, and the one most likely to produce a plan that breaks on contact with reality.

What happens

AI-assisted coding speeds up the mechanical parts, boilerplate, first drafts, syntax, which moves the bottleneck to review, architectural judgment, and testing. Those were never the slow part for a senior engineer, and they were close to the entire job for a junior one.

The naive read

Treating a rise in lines of code or pull request volume as productivity, then setting headcount or delivery expectations against that number. The plan breaks the moment the team hits the bottleneck AI never touched: review capacity and architectural decision-making.

2

Code review changes shape

Reviewing AI-generated code is a different skill than reviewing human-written code, and most engineers were never trained on the difference.

What's different

Reviewing AI-generated code means checking for plausible-but-wrong patterns, code that looks idiomatic and compiles cleanly but embeds a subtly incorrect assumption, more than checking for the kind of mistake a human engineer is likely to make. That's a distinct reviewing skill.

The risk

Review fatigue from higher volume, combined with AI-generated code's tendency to look confident and clean, produces a false sense of safety. Reviewers approve faster because the code reads well, without having verified it any more rigorously.

3

What happens to junior engineer development

The concern most tempting to dismiss, because it doesn't show up as a problem until much later.

The concern

A junior engineer who leans on AI for the mechanical work skips the repetition that used to build pattern recognition and debugging intuition the slow way. That intuition doesn't obviously appear missing until a novel problem requires it and it isn't there.

What to do about it

Deliberately assign some work without AI assistance specifically for skill-building, framed as a training decision rather than a productivity one, the same way a pilot still trains on manual flight even though autopilot handles most of the flying.

4

Measuring productivity when the tool is part of the output

The measurement question every other section on this page eventually points back to.

What doesn't work

Raw output volume: lines of code, pull requests merged, AI suggestions accepted. All of these can rise while the team ships less correct, maintained value, since none of them measure whether the output was right.

What holds up

The outcome measures already covered on Engineering Metrics, cycle time, change failure rate, and ultimately business outcomes, tracked specifically before and after AI tooling adoption, rather than assuming adoption itself is automatically the win.

What the evidence says, as of September 2026

The research is mixed in ways that matter. Gains are real on bounded tasks and harder to find on complex, familiar codebases, and quality and security do not improve on their own.

SourceFinding
GitHub controlled experiment (Peng and colleagues, 2023)Developers finished a bounded, well-specified task about 55 percent faster with an AI assistant
DORA, 2024Higher AI adoption was associated with slightly lower delivery throughput and noticeably lower delivery stability
DORA, 2025 State of AI-assisted Software DevelopmentAbout 90 percent of respondents use AI and over 80 percent report productivity gains; AI adoption was now associated with higher throughput, but instability persisted, and only about a quarter reported high trust in AI output. The report's central finding is that AI amplifies a team's existing strengths and weaknesses
METR randomized trial (July 2025)Experienced open-source maintainers working in their own large repositories took about 19 percent longer with AI tools, while believing they were faster
METR update (February 2026)A follow-up could not produce a reliable new estimate because developers increasingly declined to work without AI; the authors think gains have likely grown since early 2025 but could not measure how much
Veracode GenAI Code Security reports (2025, spring 2026)About 45 percent of AI-generated code samples failed security tests, with no material improvement from newer models; syntax correctness rose while security did not

The DORA AI Capabilities Model

DORA's 2025 research identified seven capabilities that determine whether AI helps or hurts. Most are not about AI at all, which is the point.

An AI coding policy engineers will actually follow

Short, specific, and enforced by tooling where possible. Anything that relies only on memory will not survive the first deadline.

TopicPolicy
Tools and dataOnly approved tools under enterprise terms that exclude training on your code; no customer data, secrets, or regulated data in prompts to unapproved tools
ReviewAI-assisted changes meet the same review bar as any other; very large generated diffs are split before review
SecuritySecret scanning, dependency scanning, and static analysis run on every change, because roughly half of generated code fails security checks
LicensingUse tools with duplicate-code filtering and IP indemnity; flag generated code that reproduces recognizable open-source code
AgentsCoding agents run with least privilege in sandboxed environments, never hold production credentials, and need human approval to merge or deploy
MeasurementTrack change failure rate, rework rate, and review time before and after adoption, per Engineering Metrics

Checklist

Signs AI adoption is being measured or managed naively

Patterns that show up when AI tooling gets adopted enthusiastically but without adjusting how the organization measures or trains around it.

  • Productivity claims rest on lines of code, PR volume, or AI suggestion acceptance rate rather than outcome measures
  • Code review practice hasn't been adjusted for the specific failure modes of AI-generated code
  • No deliberate mechanism exists for junior engineers to build skills without AI assistance
  • Change failure rate or defect rate hasn't been tracked before and after AI tooling adoption
  • Headcount or timeline plans assume a productivity multiplier that was asserted rather than measured
  • There is no written AI coding policy, or it exists but nothing in the pipeline enforces it
  • Coding agents run with credentials that can reach production
